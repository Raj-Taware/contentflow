The loop

Consume → think → publish.
On repeat.

Most people consume great content and let it evaporate. ContentFlow closes the loop between what goes into your head and what comes out of your keyboard.

1

Queue everything

Papers, videos, threads, podcasts, books. Add from your laptop or phone in two seconds — title, link, tags, priority. Your queue, sorted by what matters most.

2

Mark it done — it moves to the Studio

Finished something? One click and it lands in your writing pipeline. Nothing you consume gets to silently disappear anymore.

3

Dump thoughts, get connectors

Write your raw takes. Pick an angle — contrarian, story, question, listicle — and get hooks, bridges and closers built from your own words. It never writes the post for you. Your voice stays yours.

4

Schedule it. Or get nagged.

Set a date for every draft. And if you finished something three days ago without writing a word about it — ContentFlow reminds you. Consistency, enforced.

Why it's different

Built for people who'd rather
own their pipeline.

🍿

Netflix for your reading list

A visual queue with type filters, tags, search and priority. Adding something takes less time than losing the tab.

✍️

Connectors, not ghostwriting

Hooks, bridges and closers generated from your thoughts. The marketing scaffolding — without outsourcing your voice to a bot.

⏰

Anti-procrastination built in

"You read this 5 days ago and wrote nothing." The reminder you need, exactly when you need it.

📱

Phone + laptop

Runs on your machine, reachable from your phone on the same wifi. Add to home screen and it feels like a native app.

🔒

Your data, one JSON file

No accounts, no cloud, no tracking. Everything lives in a single file you can read, back up or edit by hand.

⚡

Zero dependencies

Plain Node.js standard library and vanilla JavaScript. Nothing to install, nothing to break, nothing to audit.

Get started

Running in 30 seconds.

# clone it
git clone https://github.com/Raj-Taware/contentflow.git

# run it (Node 18+, no npm install needed)
cd contentflow && node server.js

# open http://localhost:4321 — done
